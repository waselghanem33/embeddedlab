Connect components. Test your design.

Choose a board and experiment. Connect terminals by clicking a start socket and an end socket, or dragging from one socket to another. Validate the logical wiring and export your design.

This is a node-to-node connectivity teaching tool, not a physical breadboard, electrical solver, firmware runtime or verified hardware pinout.

1 · Experiment setup

2 · Wiring tools

Select a terminal to start a wire.

Click a wire to remove it. Dragging works with mouse or touch; two-click connection is also supported for keyboard-friendly operation.

3 · Interactive circuit canvas

The center area is a conceptual breadboard workspace. Connections are drawn between labeled terminals; physical breadboard rows and power rails are not electrically modeled.

4 · Engineering diagnostics

Run validation to see missing, incorrect or shorted logical connections.

5 · Save and share

Local browser storage only. No cloud synchronization or Moodle gradebook is connected.

Suggested classroom workflow

Predict → Connect → Validate → Explain → Compare. Students predict the correct circuit, wire it without hints, validate their design, explain the diagnosis and compare it with the example.

Safety: never use these diagrams as a substitute for hardware datasheets, correct supply-voltage checks, or a real current-limiting resistor. The ESP32 is generally a 3.3 V logic device.
